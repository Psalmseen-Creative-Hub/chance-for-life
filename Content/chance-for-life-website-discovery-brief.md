## 8. Contact Form — Technical Notes
The client wants form submissions delivered to **info@chanceforlifeinc.com**. A plain contact form doesn't "email itself" on its own — it needs one of these behind it:
- A form-handling service (e.g., Formspree, Web3Forms) — works on any static host, no backend needed
- Native handling from the hosting platform (e.g., Netlify Forms) — only if hosted there
- A small backend/serverless function that sends via an email API (e.g., Resend, SendGrid) — more control, more setup

## 13. Design Direction Notes (from reviewing jhearthc.com)
For reference, here's what the client's example site does structurally: sticky header with phone/email bar, dropdown services menu, large hero with mission statement, an "About" teaser section, a services grid with icons/photos, a "Why choose us" feature row, a supported-living deep-dive section, a testimonials carousel, an FAQ accordion, and a footer with full contact details and social links.

It's functional but visually generic (stock template feel, inconsistent image treatment, dense text blocks). Since you want something **more beautiful and distinctive**, the plan is to keep the *information architecture* that works (clear services, testimonials, FAQ, easy contact) while elevating the *visual execution* — stronger typography, a real color system, better image treatment, more breathing room, and a more editorial, story-led homepage rather than a services-directory feel.

## 14. Legal / Compliance
- [x] **HIPAA is a stated priority for the client** — this shapes several build decisions:
  - The general contact form should collect only name/email/phone/brief reason for contact — **not** detailed health information. Consider explicit form copy like "Please don't include personal health details in this form; we'll follow up securely" so visitors self-limit what they submit.
  - Whatever email-delivery method we use for the form (see Section 8) should not be treated as a secure PHI channel — plain form-to-email is fine for *intake interest*, not for clinical communication.
  - A **Notice of Privacy Practices** (standard for healthcare providers, separate from a normal website privacy policy) is likely needed — flag to client as a compliance item, not something to draft without her/legal input.
